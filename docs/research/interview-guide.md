# Interview Guide — Requirements Interviews I-01 … I-05

**Purpose.** Understand how students currently fit study into their day, decide what to study, learn new topics, check their knowledge, study with others and stay motivated. Test the design hypotheses H1–H10 ([02 §2.5](../02-evidence-of-relevance.md#25-verified-findings-and-design-hypotheses)) and turn the results into requirement priorities.

**Format.** Semi-structured, 25–30 minutes, one participant and one interviewer (Ayat), with an optional note-taker (Yernar). In person or by video call. Conducted in the participant's preferred language (Kazakh, Russian or English); notes are written in English.

**Participants.** 3–5 AITU students, not involved in the project, chosen for variety ([consent-and-recruitment.md](consent-and-recruitment.md)).

**Principles.**
- Ask about **specific recent episodes** ("the last time you…"), not general opinions.
- Do not describe YeahGrind before part E; do not ask leading questions ("Wouldn't it be great if…").
- Write down observable facts and quotes separately from your interpretation.
- Skip any question the participant does not want to answer.

---

## A. Opening (2 min)

Read the consent script from [consent-and-recruitment.md](consent-and-recruitment.md). Record verbal consent and whether audio recording is allowed.

1. What do you study, which year are you in, and do you work or have other regular commitments?

## B. Study windows and deciding what to study — H1, H3 (6 min)

2. Think about yesterday (or your last weekday). When did you study outside class, for how long each time, and where?
3. How did you decide what to study first? Tell me about the last time you did not know where to start.
4. Where do your deadlines come from (Moodle, messengers, teachers)? When did you last nearly miss one, and why? — H2

## C. Explanations and video — H4 (5 min)

5. When you meet a new topic, what do you open first: lecture slides, textbook, YouTube, a chatbot? Why?
6. Tell me about the last short video that really helped you understand something. What made it work? How long was it?
7. Would you trust a short explanation video generated by an AI from your own lecture notes? What would you need to see to trust it?

## D. Checking knowledge and feedback — H5, H6 (6 min)

8. How do you find out what you do not know yet before an exam? When did you last find a gap too late?
9. Do you test yourself (quizzes, flashcards, past papers)? What makes a quiz useful or annoying?
10. When you use an AI chatbot for studying, what exactly do you ask? When did that help you learn, and when did it only give you the answer?

## E. Studying with others and motivation — H7, H8 (5 min)

11. Do you study with friends or groupmates? How do you share materials and progress today?
12. Have you used apps with leaderboards, streaks or points (Duolingo, etc.)? What kept you going, and what made you stop? How did you feel when you lost a streak or dropped in a league?
13. What would you be happy to share with friends (completed lessons, videos, scores)? What would you keep private?

## F. Concept probe — H3, H4, H5 (5 min)

Show the mockups **P3 (today's micro-plan)**, **P4 (micro-unit with video)** and **P5b (quiz feedback and mastery)** from [`prototype/screens/`](../../prototype/screens/). Ask the participant to think aloud.

14. What do you think each screen does? What would you do next?
15. Is the reason shown for each planned unit useful? Is the "Sources" panel enough to trust the video?
16. Is the feedback after the quiz helpful for learning? Would you prefer to see the correct answer immediately? Why?

## G. Data and language — H9, H10 (2 min)

17. Which information would you share to get a personal plan (free time, energy, Moodle deadlines, your lecture notes, quiz answers)? What never?
18. In which language do you prefer to study and to listen to explanations?

## H. Closing (1 min)

19. If one tool could fix one thing about how you study, what would it be?
20. Is there anything I did not ask that matters to you?

Thank the participant. Remind them how to withdraw their notes.

## Note-taking and synthesis

- Use [`interview-notes/TEMPLATE.md`](interview-notes/TEMPLATE.md) and save each interview as `I-0n.md` in the team's private notes folder (not in the public repository) within 24 hours.
- Use pseudonymous IDs only (I-01 … I-05). Remove names of people, teachers and courses that could identify someone.
- **Synthesis:**
  1. Copy every observation onto a card (in the shared board or a table) and tag it with H1–H10 or a new theme N1, N2, …
  2. For each hypothesis, count the participants whose concrete examples **support** or **contradict** it.
  3. Status rule:
     - *supported*: ≥ 3 participants give supporting examples and none contradict;
     - *contradicted*: ≥ 3 contradict;
     - *mixed*: everything else;
     - new themes mentioned by ≥ 2 participants become candidate requirements.
  4. Update [02 §2.4–2.6](../02-evidence-of-relevance.md#24-interviews-with-potential-users) and the priorities in [06](../06-requirements-specification.md). Commit with task ID `YG-3`.
